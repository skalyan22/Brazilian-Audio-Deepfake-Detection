# BrazilianDF: A Metadata-Rich Benchmark for Fair and Robust Audio Deepfake Detection Across Brazilian Portuguese Speech Communities

## Collaborators: Kyra Cui, Shreya Kalyan, Rashmi Nagpal

### Audio deepfake detectors are reported to reach accuracies near 95%, however these are under controlled recording conditions and their robustness in real-world deployment is poorly characterized. We present BrazilianDF, a metadata-rich benchmark for audio deepfake detection in Brazilian Portuguese, a language with substantial regional accent variation and limited representation in existing deepfake corpora. The benchmark pairs 28,253 consented real and voice-cloned utterances from the same speakers and texts, carries per-utterance metadata (region, gender, age, codec, and synthesis engine), and defines a protocol with speaker-disjoint splits, a decision threshold frozen in-domain, and mandatory out-of-distribution, degraded-channel, fairness, and calibration reporting. Applying this protocol to two standard baselines exposes failures that single-number evaluation conceals: a mel-spectrogram CNN reaching 0.35% equal error rate in-domain degrades to 36–50% EER on unseen corpora and engines, rejects zero of 5,412 spoofs from one unseen TTS engine at its deployed operating point, and misses 99.9% of spoofs under phone-band audio despite a nearly unchanged EER; bonafide false-alarm rates further differ by up to 2× across well-sampled Brazilian macro-regions in an 89-speaker conversational sample. This work aims to shift the field’s evaluation culture from single-number accuracy claims toward benchmarks that make robustness and fairness measurable and reportable.
